# Sprint 38 Verdict
## Committee Red Team — ImpactOne

---

## Committee Independence Score: 3 / 10

Two of five members (Macro Strategist and Alternative Data Analyst) produced byte-identical confidence numbers on both tickers reviewed, and the aggregate Consensus/Disagreement split (60%/40%) was identical across both — the clearest possible sign of a shared underlying calculation rather than five independently-reasoning specialists. No individual rationale text is shown for any member, so genuine independent reasoning cannot even be verified where it might exist.

## Trust Score: 4 / 10

Genuine, real directional variety exists (never a unanimous vote), and the committee is correctly kept out of the canonical verdict per this platform's own architecture. But the observed gap between the committee's collective Reduce/Hold lean and the headline "Buy" verdict, with no visible reconciliation, actively undermines trust rather than building it once a user reads closely enough to notice.

## False-Consensus Risks

- Identical confidence numbers between two named specialists (Macro Strategist and Alternative Data Analyst) across two unrelated tickers.
- Identical aggregate Consensus/Disagreement percentage (60%/40%) across two unrelated tickers.
- No per-member reasoning text, making it impossible for a user (or this review) to distinguish genuine independent disagreement from five labels applied to a smaller number of underlying signals.

## Best Specialist

**Risk Manager** — the only member whose confidence number was distinct from every other member in both observed cases, playing a genuinely distinguishable role (downside framing) rather than echoing another persona's output.

## Weakest Specialist

**Alternative Data Analyst** — its confidence number exactly matched the Macro Strategist's in both cases observed. As currently shown, there is no evidence this persona contributes an independent signal at all.

## Is This Architecture Worthy of Becoming ImpactOne's Defining Feature?

**Not yet, as currently shown to users.** The underlying idea — a panel of specialists debating before a canonical verdict is reached, with the debate never allowed to become a second decision-maker — is genuinely sound and matches this platform's own stated architecture. But a defining feature has to survive exactly the kind of side-by-side comparison this review performed, and today it does not: two specialists produce identical numbers, the aggregate split repeats exactly across unrelated companies, and the committee's own collective lean contradicts the headline verdict with nothing shown to explain why. Before this becomes a flagship feature, the committee needs to visibly reason in its own words, per specialist, per company — and the gap between what five experts said and what the user is told to do needs to be explained, not left silent.
