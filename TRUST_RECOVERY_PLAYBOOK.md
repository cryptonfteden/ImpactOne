# Trust Recovery Playbook
## Office of the Chief Customer Officer — ImpactOne

**Mandate:** Design exactly how ImpactOne responds when things go wrong — a recommendation fails, the AI is wrong, data is missing, a provider fails, markets crash, or a user loses money. The standard for every response below is the same: **trust must be higher after the mistake is handled than it would have been if the mistake had never been visible at all.** This is not spin. It is the direct, structural consequence of handling a mistake more honestly than a customer expected any company to.

---

## The One Principle Behind Every Scenario Below

A company that hides its mistakes is trusted only as long as no one notices. A company that surfaces its mistakes, plainly, before being asked, is trusted more with every mistake that passes through that process — because each one is proof the honesty is real, not conditional on convenience. Every response in this document is built from that one mechanic, applied to a different situation.

---

## When a Recommendation Fails

**Immediate response:** The recommendation's graded outcome is marked incorrect the moment its outcome window matures — automatically, on schedule, with no manual delay or discretionary softening.

**What we show the user:** The specific call, what was expected, what actually happened, and — critically — an honest attribution of *why*, decomposed into whether the mechanism was wrong, the timing was wrong, or the evidence itself was misleading. A vague "the market didn't cooperate" is never acceptable; a specific account of what broke is.

**What we never do:** Quietly let a wrong call fade out of view. Retroactively soften the original confidence language to make the miss look more forgivable in hindsight — the original call stays exactly as it was made, and the miss is recorded plainly alongside it.

**Why this increases trust:** A user who sees a specific, undefended account of a real miss learns that every "correct" badge they've ever seen was earned the same honest way — this is the single fastest way to make a track record credible rather than suspicious.

---

## When the AI Is Wrong

**Immediate response:** The same graded-outcome process above applies, plus an explicit statement distinguishing "the AI's reasoning was flawed" from "the AI's reasoning was sound but the world did something genuinely unexpected" — these are different failures with different lessons, and conflating them teaches the wrong one.

**What we show the user:** Whichever of the two failure types actually occurred, stated plainly, without hedging language designed to blur the distinction in the platform's own favor.

**What we never do:** Blame the user for "misunderstanding" a recommendation that was, in fact, wrong. Use technical-sounding language to make a plain mistake sound more sophisticated or excusable than it was.

**Why this increases trust:** Users forgive a system that is honest about being an imperfect, probabilistic tool. They do not forgive a system that quietly implies its errors were somehow their fault, or dresses up a wrong answer in language designed to sound less wrong.

---

## When Data Is Missing

**Immediate response:** Any claim, score, or recommendation resting on incomplete data is labeled as such, visibly, at the moment it's shown — never silently defaulted to a "reasonable-looking" placeholder value.

**What we show the user:** Exactly what's missing and why (a provider outage, a data gap for a specific security), and how that affects the confidence of what's shown — a missing data point should visibly lower confidence or uncertainty scores, never be quietly ignored as if it had no effect.

**What we never do:** Fill a gap with a plausible-sounding synthetic value presented as if it were real. Show a fully-confident-looking screen when a material input is actually absent.

**Why this increases trust:** "We don't have enough information to say" is a complete, respectable, and rare answer in this entire industry — a user who sees it delivered honestly learns that every fully-confident answer they do see was actually backed by real, complete data.

---

## When Providers Fail

**Immediate response:** A graceful, honestly-labeled fallback state appears immediately — cached data with a visible staleness label, or an explicit "temporarily unavailable" message — never a blank screen, a raw error, or a silent retry loop with no explanation.

**What we show the user:** Which specific capability is degraded, since when, and what the user can still reliably do in the meantime.

**What we never do:** Present stale data as if it were current. Let a provider outage silently degrade the quality of a recommendation without disclosing that degradation at the point of use.

**Why this increases trust:** Infrastructure failures are inevitable in any real system; a user's trust is determined entirely by whether the failure is disclosed honestly in the moment or discovered later by accident. The former builds trust even during an outage; the latter destroys it the moment it's found.

---

## When Markets Crash

**Immediate response:** A calm, specific, promptly-delivered explanation of what's happening and what it means for this specific user's actual holdings — not a generic market-wide alert blasted to everyone with identical, undifferentiated urgency.

**What we show the user:** What's known, what's genuinely uncertain, and — where the platform has relevant historical data — how similar past regimes actually resolved, presented as a real, honest data point, never as false reassurance. Confidence and uncertainty scores during a crash are held to exactly the same honesty standard as during a calm market — if genuine uncertainty is high, the platform says so plainly, especially now.

**What we never do:** Increase notification frequency or urgency language during a crash beyond what the actual, personally-relevant facts justify. Offer blanket reassurance ("this will definitely recover") stated as fact rather than as a scored, honest view with its own confidence and invalidation condition. Go silent, which reads as either incompetence or indifference at the exact moment a user needs the platform most.

**Why this increases trust:** This is the single highest-leverage trust moment the company will ever have, precisely because it's the moment every competitor is most likely to either panic-monetize or go quiet. A calm, honest, specifically-relevant response here is worth more to the long-term relationship than a hundred ordinary calm-market days combined.

---

## When Users Lose Money

**Immediate response:** No response minimizes, reframes, or rushes past the loss. The platform's own record on this specific call or period is surfaced honestly alongside the loss, whether the platform's own recommendations contributed to it or not.

**What we show the user:** A clear, honest accounting of what happened to their specific holdings, how it compares to a relevant benchmark, and — if a platform recommendation was involved — the same graded, attributed honesty described above for a failed recommendation.

**What we never do:** Suggest the loss is the user's fault for "not following the recommendation closely enough." Use the moment to upsell a feature, a premium tier, or additional engagement. Go quiet and let the user process a real financial loss with no acknowledgment from the platform they were using.

**Why this increases trust:** A user who has genuinely lost money is at their most emotionally exposed and most likely to remember, permanently, exactly how the platform behaved in that moment. Behaving with restraint, honesty, and real acknowledgment — with zero attempt to extract value from the moment — is the clearest possible demonstration that the platform's stated principles (never optimizing for engagement over outcomes, risk disclosed with the same weight as upside) are real commitments, not marketing copy.

---

## The Standing Discipline Behind All Six Scenarios

Every scenario above follows the same four-step shape: **surface it before being asked, explain it specifically rather than generically, attribute it honestly rather than defensively, and never use the moment to extract engagement, attention, or revenue.** A company that can be trusted to follow this same shape every single time — for a missing data point as much as for a market crash — is a company whose mistakes become the strongest evidence for its own trustworthiness, not the weakest.
