# Daily Value Framework
## Office of the Global Product Board — ImpactOne

**Mandate:** Define what actually makes a user return every morning, separated into four components that must all be present simultaneously — missing any one collapses the entire loop, regardless of how strong the other three are.

---

## The Four Components

### Habit
**What it is:** The mechanical, low-friction repeatability of the experience — same short duration, roughly the same time, roughly the same shape every day.
**What it requires:** The 90-second morning experience (`MORNING_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT.md`), a user-chosen notification time, and zero variance in how long or how effortful the daily check feels.
**How it's measured:** Daily open rate and session-length consistency.

### Trust
**What it is:** The belief that what's shown is honest and worth checking at all.
**What it requires:** Confidence and uncertainty shown honestly and independently, real evidence behind every claim, and a track record that includes the misses.
**How it's measured:** The Trust Score's four components (`TRUST_SCORE_MODEL.md`) — Stated, Behavioral, Resilience, Advocacy.

### Learning
**What it is:** Something genuinely new understood or reinforced, even in a two-minute session.
**What it requires:** Contextual, fading explanations (`EDUCATION_STRATEGY.md`) and content that teaches pattern recognition over time, not just headlines.
**How it's measured:** Comprehension growth (`DECISION_QUALITY_MODEL.md`) — fewer repeated "explain this" taps, more independent evidence engagement over time.

### Actionability
**What it is:** A clear, honest answer to "do I need to do anything today" — including a genuine, un-padded "no."
**What it requires:** One primary answer per session, never a list requiring the user to decide what matters most themselves, and an honest quiet day treated as a valid, complete answer.
**How it's measured:** Percentage of sessions that end in a clear, satisfied close with no further navigation required (`MOBILE_ACCEPTANCE_SCORECARD.md` #11).

---

## Why All Four Must Be Present Together

- **Habit without Trust** produces anxious, compulsive checking — a user who opens the app daily out of worry, not confidence, is not experiencing daily value, they're experiencing a dependency this platform explicitly refuses to manufacture.
- **Trust without Actionability** produces a well-regarded report nobody needs to open — a user who believes the platform but never gets a clear answer to "what now" will eventually stop checking, because trust alone doesn't justify the daily interruption.
- **Actionability without Learning** produces a user who never becomes more capable — useful in the short term, but it caps the relationship at "useful tool" and forecloses the multi-year arc `LONG_TERM_RETENTION.md` depends on.
- **Learning without Habit** produces genuine understanding that never gets reinforced daily — valuable in isolated moments, but it never compounds into the calibrated intuition this platform is actually trying to build.

**Daily value is the product of all four, not the sum.** A component scoring near zero drags the whole experience down regardless of how strong the other three are — this is why the framework tracks all four separately and never allows a strong score in one to be reported as compensating for a weak score in another.

---

## Applying This Framework Today

Per the most recent live product review, Habit and Actionability show real, recent improvement (a working mobile layout, a clear Home answer, honest empty states). Trust remains compromised by a specific, still-open finding (a false personalized claim shown against an empty test account) — and per the rule above, that single open Trust failure caps the *entire* daily-value experience, regardless of how well Habit and Actionability are now performing. This framework's purpose is exactly to make that kind of imbalance visible and undeniable, rather than letting genuine progress in three components quietly excuse a real failure in the fourth.
