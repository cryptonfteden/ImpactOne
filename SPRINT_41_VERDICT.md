## Trust Consistency Score: Not measurable today — 0 / 10 by default

A score cannot honestly be assigned above zero for a product that could not be reached for the entire review session. Assigning any higher number would be an assumption, not evidence.

## Recommendation Consistency Score: Unchanged from the last verified session — 3 / 10

Carried forward, explicitly dated, from the prior session's confirmed finding (both TSLA and NVDA showing an all-Reduce/Hold committee under a "Buy" headline, with no reconciliation shown). Not re-verified today; reported as the most recent real evidence available, not as today's result.

## Remaining Contradictions

- The TSLA/NVDA committee-versus-headline contradiction from the prior session remains unresolved and unverified either way today.
- AAPL and SPY have never been tested for this pattern in any session — an open gap, not a clean result.
- A new, more severe contradiction as of today: the product's own console shows it silently retrying a service that has been down for days, with no user-facing acknowledgment that anything is wrong — the same "silent failure on some screens, honest disclosure on others" inconsistency flagged in the previous session, now observed persisting across a multi-day outage rather than a momentary one.

## Can This Product Now Be Trusted Internally by the First Five Beta Users?

**No — and not for the reason this sprint set out to test.** The question this sprint was built to answer (does the product agree with itself) cannot even be asked of a product that has been unreachable for three consecutive review sessions. Before any question about internal consistency is worth asking again, the product needs to demonstrably be *up* — reliably, for real users, for more than a few minutes at a time. That is a more basic and more urgent bar than anything else in this report, and it has not been cleared.
