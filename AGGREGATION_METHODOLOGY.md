# Aggregation Methodology — Ensemble, Voting, Calibration, and Bayesian Approaches for Unified Stock Intelligence

**Phase:** UNIFIED-SCORING-RESEARCH-001. Pure research — no production code was written. Grounds every recommendation in this platform's **real, already-shipped** aggregation layer: `backend/services/agentOrchestrator/agentOrchestrator.js`'s `rankByConfidence()`, `mergeEvidence()`, `detectConflicts()`, and `computeOverallConfidence()` — all four read directly from source for this research, all four confirmed real and tested. This document evaluates the requested aggregation paradigms against that real foundation and the platform's now-5-agent-strong real registry (Technical, Options, Sentiment, Valuation, Earnings — all real as of commits `72a6129` through `585a715`), then recommends what to build next, in `UNIFIED_SCORING_MODEL.md` and `CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md`.

---

## 1. What already exists, and why it matters for choosing a methodology

Unlike the Options/Valuation/Algorithmic-Activity research phases (each starting from an honest stub with zero prior implementation), this phase starts from **real, running aggregation code** with real behavior to evaluate, not just a blank slate:

- `computeOverallConfidence()` is a **priority-weighted average of each fulfilled agent's own `confidence()`** — a simple, transparent weighted-mean ensemble, already real.
- `detectConflicts()` is a **pairwise structural comparison of raw `direction` strings** — a genuine disagreement-surfacing mechanism, already real, and — per this research's own direct verification — **already exposes a real gap**: every one of the 5 real agents uses its **own, independent direction vocabulary** (Technical: `UPTREND`/`DOWNTREND`/`MIXED`; Options: `BULLISH`/`BEARISH`; Sentiment: `STABLE`/`IMPROVING`/`DETERIORATING` — notably a *rate-of-change-of-sentiment* concept, not a bullish/bearish-level concept at all; Valuation: `UNDERVALUED`/`OVERVALUED`/`FAIRLY_VALUED`; Earnings: a guidance-direction-derived outlook). Confirmed by direct source read across all 5 agent files and their underlying services. Since `detectConflicts()` compares these **raw strings for exact equality**, two agents that actually *agree* (e.g., Technical's `UPTREND` and Options' `BULLISH` both mean "bullish") are reported as a structural conflict today, purely because their strings differ — a real, present, verifiable false-positive-conflict risk in the shipped code. This single finding is the central, evidence-grounded motivation for `CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md`'s canonical-direction-taxonomy proposal.
- The platform already has a **proven, real precedent for aggregating multiple pieces of evidence into one number without hiding disagreement**, one layer below the agent level: the Claim Intelligence Layer's `claimConfidence.js` (`aggregateConfidence()`/`aggregateProbability()`, confirmed real and tested per this engagement's own `AI_CORE_001_REVIEW`), which shares **zero input variables** between its confidence and probability computations, and applies a **dominance cap** (`MAX_SINGLE_EVIDENCE_WEIGHT = 0.4`, via `capAndRedistributeWeights()`) so that no single piece of evidence — however strong — can silently dominate a Claim's aggregate confidence. This is the direct, real conceptual ancestor this research recommends extending **one level up**, from "multiple evidence items → one Claim" to "multiple agent reports → one Unified Intelligence Report," rather than inventing an unrelated aggregation scheme.

## 2. Ensemble models — evaluated

**Concept:** combine multiple independent models/predictors, typically by averaging or voting, on the well-established statistical premise that a diverse ensemble's aggregate error is usually lower than any single member's.

- **Fit for this platform:** strong, and already partially real — `computeOverallConfidence()`'s weighted average **is** a simple ensemble-averaging scheme. The 5 real agents are genuinely diverse in methodology and data source (price/volume technicals, options flow, social/news sentiment, fundamentals-based valuation, earnings-guidance direction) — a textbook case for ensemble diversity actually paying off, not an artificially manufactured diversity.
- **What this research recommends adding, not replacing:** a real ensemble should weight members not just by a static `priority` constant (the current mechanism) but by **demonstrated reliability** once real graded-outcome history exists (reusing the platform's own already-built `Outcome`/`calibrationReportService.js` infrastructure — the same "don't invent a second calibration mechanism" principle already recommended in `OPTIONS_SCORING_MODEL.md` §2.2 and `VALUATION_SCORING_MODEL.md` §1.3). At MVP, with no such history yet for this specific unified-report level, **static, disclosed, hand-set priority weights remain the right choice** — consistent with this whole engagement's "don't calibrate against too small a sample" discipline.
- **Not recommended:** a fitted/opaque stacking ensemble (a meta-model trained to weight the base agents) — this would be the first genuinely non-explainable component in this platform's entire scoring stack, directly contradicting the hand-set/disclosed convention every other scoring model in this engagement (`optionsAnomalyConfidence.js`, `scoringVocabulary.js`'s `QUALITY_WEIGHTS`) has deliberately chosen instead.

## 3. Weighted voting — evaluated

**Concept:** each member "votes" for a direction/outcome, weighted by some measure of that member's importance or reliability; the aggregate is the weighted tally, not a simple average of continuous scores.

- **Fit for this platform:** **this is, in effect, exactly what `detectConflicts()` plus a canonical-direction layer would become** — each agent casts a "vote" for BULLISH/BEARISH/NEUTRAL (after normalization, per `CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md`), weighted by `priority × confidence`. Recommend this as the mechanism underlying the **Agreement Bonus** and **Conflict Penalty** terms defined in `UNIFIED_SCORING_MODEL.md` — a natural, well-understood extension of the real, already-shipped structural-conflict-detection mechanism, not a new paradigm bolted on top of it.
- **Key distinction to preserve, explicitly:** a vote tally answers "which direction has more weight of evidence," which is a **useful, different question from** "how much do we trust the underlying data overall" (answered by Overall Confidence) — these must remain two distinct outputs, never collapsed into one number, mirroring the Claim Layer's confidence-vs-probability separation and the Algorithmic Activity Score's magnitude-vs-`ExecutionPressure`-direction separation.

## 4. Confidence calibration — evaluated

**Concept:** ensuring that a stated confidence value (e.g., "80% confident") actually corresponds, empirically, to being right roughly 80% of the time — the discipline of checking a model's stated confidence against its real track record, not just its internal consistency.

- **Fit for this platform:** **not yet achievable at the unified-report level** — this requires real graded outcomes (did the report's overall lean turn out correct) accumulated over time, exactly the same precondition already identified as missing for weight-fitting in §2. The platform's own `Outcome`/`WorldMemoryPrediction`/`calibrationReportService.js` infrastructure is the correct, already-built mechanism to eventually calibrate against — recommend explicitly wiring a **new, `Recommendation`-independent grading path** for "was the Unified Intelligence Report's overall lean directionally correct" once enough real report history exists, reusing (not duplicating) the existing calibration service's own sample-size-gating discipline (`MIN_SAMPLE_SIZE`).
- **What IS achievable now, and recommended:** **internal consistency calibration** — ensuring that within the platform's own hand-set scoring rules, a "90 confidence" report is *structurally* required to reflect more agreement, more coverage, and fresher signals than a "60 confidence" one (i.e., the formula's own internal logic is coherent and monotonic in the right directions), even before any external, outcome-based calibration is possible. This is what `UNIFIED_SCORING_MODEL.md`'s formulas are designed to guarantee.

## 5. Conflict resolution — evaluated as a methodology (see also `CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md` for the full, dedicated treatment)

**Concept, as a family of aggregation approaches:** ranges from simple majority-rule (whichever direction has the most votes wins, disagreement is discarded) to full disagreement-preserving approaches (the aggregate output explicitly retains and surfaces the fact that disagreement existed, rather than resolving it away).

- **Explicitly rejected approach:** majority-rule vote-and-discard — this would be a direct violation of this mission's own explicit requirement ("explain how multiple agents should combine **without hiding disagreements**") and of this platform's own long-established, hard-won principle (`IMPACTONE_ANTI_PATTERNS.md`'s "contradiction between two evidence-backed claims is surfaced, not silently resolved/averaged/suppressed," already proven in the Claim Layer's real `CONTRADICTS` evidence-stance handling).
- **Recommended approach:** disagreement-preserving aggregation — the Unified Intelligence Report **always** carries both (a) a single headline composite (Overall Intelligence Score/Overall Confidence, for a quick read) **and** (b) the full, unresolved, itemized conflict list (already real today, via `detectConflicts()`'s output, which this research recommends *upgrading* in accuracy via canonical-direction normalization, never *removing* or *hiding*). The headline number is a *summary*, never a *replacement*, for the itemized disagreement — exactly the same "summary is always checkable against its real inputs" principle the real `computeOverallConfidence()` code comment already states as its own design intent.

## 6. Score normalization — evaluated

**Concept:** transforming heterogeneous scores (different scales, different distributions, different meanings) onto one common, comparable scale before combining them.

- **Real, present need confirmed by this research:** the 5 real agents' own `confidence()` functions all already return a 0-100 scale (confirmed by direct source read of each agent file) — this is a genuine, already-real point of consistency, likely a deliberate design choice from `AGENT-ORCHESTRATOR-001`'s original agent-interface contract. **The genuine normalization gap is not in the confidence scale, but in the *direction* vocabulary** (§1's finding) — recommend the normalization effort be concentrated there (a canonical direction taxonomy, `CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md` §2), not spent re-normalizing a confidence scale that is already consistent.
- **A second, real, more subtle normalization concern:** not every agent's `confidence()` necessarily *means* the same thing internally, even though they share a numeric range — e.g., the Valuation Agent's confidence (per `VALUATION_SCORING_MODEL.md`'s design, now real) reflects data-completeness/method-agreement/peer-group-quality/earnings-quality, while the Options Agent's confidence (per `OPTIONS_SCORING_MODEL.md`, and the real `optionsAnomalyConfidence.js`) reflects classification-strength/size/OI-confirmation. These are **not interchangeable meanings of "confidence,"** even on the same 0-100 scale — this is a real, disclosed limitation of any weighted-average approach across agents, and should be stated explicitly in `UNIFIED_SCORING_MODEL.md` rather than silently assumed away.

## 7. Bayesian aggregation — evaluated

**Concept:** treating each agent's output as evidence that updates a prior probability distribution over the "true" state (e.g., is this stock undervalued), formally combining likelihoods rather than just averaging scores; correlated/non-independent evidence sources should contribute less combined information than the same number of truly independent sources.

- **The one Bayesian *principle* this research recommends adopting, without adopting the full Bayesian machinery:** **independence-awareness in the Agreement Bonus** (`UNIFIED_SCORING_MODEL.md` §4) — two agents whose underlying data sources substantially overlap (e.g., if a future agent derived from the same price/volume feed the Technical Agent already uses) should not receive full "independent corroboration" credit when they happen to agree, exactly the Bayesian intuition that correlated evidence provides less *new* information than independent evidence, even though both agree. This directly extends the Claim Layer's own dominance-cap precedent (§1) to the cross-agent level.
- **Full Bayesian posterior updating (an explicit prior distribution, formal likelihood functions per agent, mathematically combined via Bayes' rule) is explicitly NOT recommended for this platform at this stage:**
  1. It requires each agent to express its output as a real likelihood/probability distribution, not just a point confidence score — a significant redesign of every existing agent's interface, not an additive change.
  2. It would be **meaningfully less explainable to a non-technical retail user** than the current hand-set weighted-sum-with-named-adjustments approach — directly conflicting with this platform's own stated audience and "financial literacy company" positioning (`COMPANY_STRATEGY_REVIEW.md`).
  3. It requires a genuine prior — a real methodological question (a prior over what, exactly — the report's eventual real-world direction? a specific return magnitude?) that has no principled, uncontroversial default and would introduce a real, hard-to-explain source of hidden influence over every report, in tension with `TRUTH.md`'s epistemic standards.
- **Recommended instead:** the **Bayesian-inspired-but-explainable** design already emerging from this section — independence-discounted agreement, disclosed hand-set weights, and correlated-evidence caps — capturing the single most useful Bayesian insight (correlated evidence should count for less) without adopting the full formal apparatus.

## 8. Expert-system approaches — evaluated

**Concept:** a rule-based system where domain-expert-authored if-then rules combine inputs into a conclusion, with an explicit, inspectable rule trace (as opposed to a statistical/learned model).

- **Fit for this platform: this is, in substance, already the platform's dominant paradigm**, confirmed across every scoring model this engagement has reviewed or designed — `optionsAnomalyConfidence.js`'s named, hand-set adjustment terms; `scoringVocabulary.js`'s disclosed `QUALITY_WEIGHTS`; the Valuation Agent's real, disclosed `excludedMethods`/profile-dependent weighting rules; and the Algorithmic Activity Score's disclosed per-signal defensibility ceilings. **Recommend explicitly naming and continuing this as the deliberate paradigm for Unified Scoring too**, rather than treating it as an implicit default — an expert-system-style, disclosed-rule aggregation is the single most consistent thread across every scoring model this whole engagement has ever designed for this platform, and is the correct choice specifically because it keeps every number traceable to a real, statable reason, matching `decisionTraceExplainabilityService.js`'s own explainability precedent.

---

## 9. Summary recommendation

**Adopt a disclosed, rule-based (expert-system-style) weighted-voting ensemble**, extending the real, already-shipped `computeOverallConfidence()`/`detectConflicts()` mechanism rather than replacing it, with three concrete additions (each fully specified in `UNIFIED_SCORING_MODEL.md` and `CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md`):

1. A **canonical direction taxonomy** normalizing every agent's own vocabulary before any agreement/conflict comparison — directly fixing the real false-positive-conflict risk this research found in the shipped `detectConflicts()` code.
2. A **Conflict Penalty** and **Agreement Bonus**, both disclosed, hand-set, bounded formulas (not fitted/opaque), incorporating a Bayesian-inspired independence discount so correlated agreement is not over-credited.
3. **Signal Freshness decay and missing-data/coverage-ratio handling**, extending the existing `fulfilled`/`unavailable`/`failed` summary counts (already real in `run()`'s output) into an explicit weighting/capping mechanism rather than a purely informational side note.

Full outcome-based calibration and any weight-fitting are explicitly deferred until real graded history exists for the unified-report level specifically, reusing (never duplicating) this platform's existing `Outcome`/`calibrationReportService.js` infrastructure once that time comes.
