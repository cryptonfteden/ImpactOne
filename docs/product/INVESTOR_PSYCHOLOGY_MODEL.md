# Investor Psychology Model
## Office of the Chief Product Vision Officer — ImpactOne

**Mandate:** Model how long-term investors actually think — not how a textbook says they should think — separated into four stages of experience. A product that speaks to all four the same way is speaking to none of them correctly. This model exists so every future product, education, and communication decision can be checked against the specific psychology of the specific person it's actually reaching.

---

## Beginners

**Mental model:** Binary — a stock is "good" or "bad," a day is "up" or "down." Price is mistaken for value; a falling price feels like a mistake to be corrected, not information to be evaluated.
**Core fear:** Being embarrassed by their own ignorance — more afraid of asking a "stupid question" than of making a bad decision.
**What builds trust:** Being told, plainly, when something is genuinely uncertain — because it's the opposite of every other confident-sounding source they've encountered, and it removes the fear that they're the only one who doesn't understand.
**What breaks trust:** Jargon with no explanation. Any hint of being talked down to. A confident-sounding claim that turns out wrong with no acknowledgment.
**How they process uncertainty:** Poorly, by default — uncertainty reads as the product not knowing its job, unless it is explicitly reframed as honesty rather than incompetence.
**Common biases at this stage:** Recency bias (chasing what's hot right now), authority bias (trusting whoever sounds most confident), herd behavior (following visible crowd sentiment because they have no independent framework yet).
**What ImpactOne must never do:** Assume prior knowledge of any term without an inline explanation. Present a confident verdict with no accessible reasoning behind it — a beginner has no independent way to sanity-check a bare number.

---

## Intermediate

**Mental model:** Has a loose framework (diversification, "buy and hold," maybe an index-fund habit) but applies it inconsistently under pressure. Starting to develop a personal "story" about why they hold what they hold.
**Core fear:** Being the person who "should have known better" — this stage is acutely aware that mistakes are now their own responsibility, not just inexperience.
**What builds trust:** A platform that helps them notice when their own reasoning is inconsistent, without being condescending about it — surfacing a contradiction gently rather than declaring them wrong.
**What breaks trust:** Generic advice that doesn't seem to account for what they've already told the platform about their own goals — this stage is old enough to notice when personalization isn't real.
**How they process uncertainty:** Selectively — this is the stage most vulnerable to confirmation bias, where uncertainty about a position they already like gets minimized and uncertainty about a position they're skeptical of gets amplified.
**Common biases at this stage:** Confirmation bias, mild overconfidence after a small number of good calls, early FOMO from comparing their own results to others'.
**What ImpactOne must never do:** Reinforce a user's existing narrative just because it's what they seem to want to hear — the platform's evidence must remain identical whether it confirms or contradicts what the user already believes.

---

## Advanced

**Mental model:** Has a real, internally consistent framework (value investing, macro-driven, factor-based, or similar) and applies it with genuine discipline most of the time. Thinks in terms of risk/reward explicitly, not just price direction.
**Core fear:** Being wrong in a way that reveals their framework itself was flawed, not just unlucky — this stage has more identity invested in being "right for the right reasons" than any other.
**What builds trust:** Real methodological transparency — a full evidence chain, a quality-score breakdown, an honest invalidation condition — because this user is sophisticated enough to evaluate the reasoning itself, not just the conclusion.
**What breaks trust:** Any hint of a "black box" — a confident number with no visible derivation is treated with active suspicion at this stage, not passive acceptance.
**How they process uncertainty:** Analytically, but with strong anchoring — once this user has committed to a thesis, new contradicting evidence has to work harder to be taken seriously, a bias this stage is often unaware it still carries despite its sophistication.
**Common biases at this stage:** Overconfidence in a framework that has "worked" so far, over-trading or over-optimizing, anchoring on a personally-held thesis even against strong new evidence.
**What ImpactOne must never do:** Offer a simplified, beginner-style verdict with no depth available underneath it — this user will look for the "why," and if it isn't there, they will assume it doesn't exist.

---

## Professionals

**Mental model:** Process- and benchmark-driven. Thinks in terms of base rates, statistical significance, and attribution — a single anecdote is data, not proof, and a single good call means nothing without a track record around it.
**Core fear:** Wasting scarce time and professional credibility on a source that turns out to be unrigorous — this stage's tolerance for being wrong once, then discovering the platform hides it, is the lowest of all four.
**What builds trust:** A real, honestly-graded, decomposed track record — this is the only stage where the track record itself, not any single interaction, is the primary trust-building surface.
**What breaks trust:** Any inconsistency between two numbers on the same screen, any templated content, any confidence score that doesn't visibly vary with real evidence — this is the persona most likely to catch exactly the failure modes already found in this platform's own live audits, and the fastest to disengage permanently once they do.
**How they process uncertainty:** Rigorously, and they expect the platform to do the same — a professional user reads an uncertainty score the way they'd read a confidence interval in their own work, and will notice immediately if it doesn't behave like one.
**Common biases at this stage:** Lower personal bias, but higher institutional skepticism of consumer-grade tools generally — the burden of proof this user requires before trusting anything is the highest of the four.
**What ImpactOne must never do:** Market to this persona with confidence-first language — this is the one segment where leading with a fully decomposed, honestly-limited methodology wins more trust than leading with a confident verdict ever could.

---

## The One Principle That Applies to All Four

Every stage above is defined by a different relationship to uncertainty — beginners fear it, intermediates selectively ignore it, advanced users under-update against it, and professionals demand it be rigorously quantified. **The product's underlying evidence, confidence, and uncertainty never change across these four stages — only the depth, framing, and vocabulary used to present the same truth does**, exactly as `PERSONALIZATION_PRINCIPLES.md` requires. This model exists to make sure that personalization is actually informed by real psychological differences, not just demographic guesswork.
