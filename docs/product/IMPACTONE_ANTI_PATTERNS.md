# ImpactOne Anti-Patterns — Phase DIFFERENTIATION-001

UX and product patterns ImpactOne must never adopt, regardless of how standard or successful they are elsewhere in the category — each one is excluded because it directly contradicts the honesty and calm discipline that is the platform's actual differentiation, not because of taste alone.

## 1. Universal green-up/red-down urgency coloring
Reserving saturated, alarm-toned color for every directional data point (as every competitor reviewed does by default) manufactures urgency the underlying data frequently doesn't support. Color intensity must be earned by real, graded significance — never applied automatically to every number that moved.

## 2. Countdown timers, flashing states, and streak/gamification mechanics
Any device whose purpose is to manufacture urgency or compulsive return-visits — a countdown to "act now," a login streak, a flashing "urgent" badge with no real underlying materiality threshold — is explicitly incompatible with a platform whose stated goal is to help users need to check in less, not more.

## 3. Engagement-optimized infinite scroll
A feed designed to maximize time-on-app rather than task completion is the opposite of "zero prompt, bounded, calm." Any list on this platform must have a real, disclosed ceiling, not an algorithmically-extended stream tuned for attention capture.

## 4. The unsourced "black box" rating
A single confidence/quality number presented with no accompanying, real, inspectable evidence (the pattern common to generic quant-rating products) is exactly the kind of "AI score without a why" this platform's entire reasoning architecture exists to avoid. A rating with nothing beneath it is never permitted to appear as a finding.

## 5. Ambient, meaningless motion
Auto-playing animation, looping visual flourishes, or motion whose only purpose is to look alive — rather than to communicate that something real changed — must never be added. Stillness, by design, communicates "the thinking is done"; constant motion communicates the opposite.

## 6. Re-engagement-tuned notification frequency
Push notifications, alerts, or badges tuned to a cadence that maximizes app opens rather than genuine materiality thresholds are explicitly excluded — every notification must be justified by a real, disclosed threshold being crossed, never by a growth team's target open-rate.

## 7. Certainty-inflating language
Marketing-register phrases like "Strong Buy!", "Don't miss out!", or "This could be huge" manufacture urgency and certainty through word choice alone, independent of what the underlying confidence and evidence actually support. Copy must never claim more certainty than the platform's own real confidence score does.

## 8. Organizing the product around internal feature/data-source boundaries
A navigation structure organized as "here is our options tab, here is our news tab, here is our sentiment tab" (rather than around the user's real questions) reflects how the product was built, not what the user needs — every surface must be organized around a real question a user has, never around which internal engine produced the data.

## 9. Silent overwrite of a prior verdict or rating
Any pattern where a rating, score, or recommendation simply changes with no visible trace of what it was before and why it changed is explicitly forbidden — this is the exact opposite of the "visible belief revision" experience that is one of this platform's core differentiators (see `IMPACTONE_SIGNATURE_MOMENTS.md`, Moment 1).

## 10. Social-proof-as-evidence mechanics
Trending-ticker lists driven by popularity, follower counts, likes, or "most-viewed" rankings substitute crowd attention for real evidence, and are explicitly excluded — everything this platform ranks must be ranked by real, disclosed relevance and confidence, never by how many other users happened to look at it.
