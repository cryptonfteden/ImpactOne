# Founder Beta Simulation — Phase E3

I'm a first-time user. I signed up for the beta because a friend said "it's like having an investment committee in your pocket." I open the link on a Tuesday morning before the market opens.

## Landing

There's no landing page — I go straight into a short onboarding form asking my age and a few quick taps. No "here's what this is" moment before it starts asking me things.

- **What do I immediately understand?** This is some kind of personalized investing tool, and it wants to know a little about me before it shows me anything.
- **What confuses me?** I never got a "here's what ImpactOne does" pitch. I'm mid-form before I've decided if I trust this.
- **What would make me lose trust?** If the form felt long or asked for something invasive. It doesn't — it's short. Fine, not a problem yet.
- **What would make me come back tomorrow?** Nothing yet — I haven't seen the product.

## Dashboard (Home)

Onboarding finishes and I land on a "Morning Brief" — six cards: what happened, today-for-you, portfolio, beliefs, recommendations, a timeline. A small welcome card pops up first, telling me plainly: recommendations show up only when the engine finds something real, my portfolio is simulated paper trading, nothing here trades on my behalf. I tap "Got it."

- **What do I immediately understand?** This is a market-briefing product with a point of view, not just a data dashboard. The welcome card set my expectations honestly before I could get confused.
- **What confuses me?** A couple of the six cards are thin — "no change vs. yesterday," "no open positions" — because I'm brand new. It reads a little empty, but at least it's telling me the truth instead of padding with fake content.
- **What would make me lose trust?** If any of those empty cards had suspiciously specific fake numbers. They don't — they say "no data" plainly.
- **What would make me come back tomorrow?** The "Morning Brief" framing itself. If this actually updates every morning with something real, that's a habit I can see forming — assuming there's something to say.

## Recommendations

I click into Recommendations. Today, there are actually five: AVGO, GOOGL, NVDA, MSFT, AAPL, all marked REDUCE, all with real reasoning — a specific quality score breakdown (momentum, opportunity, risk), a real current price, real conviction language ("no single dominant news event — score of 44/100"). Every one is a REDUCE, not a mix of buys and sells.

- **What do I immediately understand?** Each recommendation is showing its work — not just "sell AVGO" but the actual scoring inputs and a real price. That's more transparent than most tools I've used.
- **What confuses me?** Everything is REDUCE. If I didn't know why (I don't, as a user — I have no idea my portfolio got concentrated in tech), I'd wonder if the engine only knows how to say "sell." A first-time user with a diversified starting portfolio might never see a BUY at all and start to doubt the tool actually finds opportunities, not just risks.
- **What would make me lose trust?** If I'd landed here with zero positions and zero recommendations and just the empty state — "run the engine or wait for a scheduled pass" — with no sense of when. That's the honest default state for a truly fresh account, and it's a real risk: my very first impression of the "brain" of the product could be an empty box.
- **What would make me come back tomorrow?** The reasoning quality, if it holds up. "Score of 44/100 reflects momentum 16/100, opportunity 63/100, risk 84/100" is the kind of specific, falsifiable claim that either earns trust over a few days or loses it fast if it turns out to be noise.

## Portfolio

I open Portfolio. It's clearly labeled simulated paper trading, starting capital $100,000. I see real positions with real entry prices, a "Place order" form, and a Reset button that asks me to confirm before wiping everything.

- **What do I immediately understand?** This is a sandbox, not my real money — clearly and repeatedly stated, which I appreciate.
- **What confuses me?** Nothing major. It's the most straightforward screen so far.
- **What would make me lose trust?** If "Place order" silently failed or hung with no feedback. It doesn't — there's a visible "Placing..." state and error text if something goes wrong.
- **What would make me come back tomorrow?** Watching a simulated position move with real market prices is genuinely satisfying — it's the closest thing to a game-like feedback loop in the product.

## AI Analysis

I search a ticker I actually hold. The screen pulls together several angles at once — analysis, committee-style breakdown, comparisons, alt-data signals — each with its own loading and error handling, so if one source is slow the whole page doesn't hang.

- **What do I immediately understand?** This is the "deep dive" screen — more analysis than I could produce myself in the same five minutes.
- **What confuses me?** There's a lot on one page. As a new user I don't yet know which of these sections to trust most or check first.
- **What would make me lose trust?** If two sections visibly contradicted each other with no acknowledgment. I don't hit that today, but with this many independent data sources feeding one page, it's a real risk over time.
- **What would make me come back tomorrow?** If this became "my" screen for the 3-4 tickers I actually care about, checked every morning before the open — plausible, if the content stays sharp.

## Settings

I open Settings expecting to tune something. There's a real language picker. Below that, "Appearance" and "Notifications" are labeled "not yet configurable," with a one-line note that these are current beta defaults.

- **What do I immediately understand?** What's real (language) and what's a placeholder (everything else) — because it says so directly.
- **What confuses me?** Nothing — this is the one screen that actively prevents confusion instead of causing it.
- **What would make me lose trust?** If those sections looked like working toggles that silently did nothing when I clicked them. They don't — this is the honest version.
- **What would make me come back tomorrow?** Not really a "come back" screen — it's infrastructure, not content. That's fine.

## Exit

There's no sign-out, no account — I just close the tab. Nothing to reconcile, nothing lost, because there was never a login to begin with.

- **What do I immediately understand?** My data is just... here, tied to this browser. Not explicitly explained anywhere.
- **What confuses me?** If I switch devices, do I lose everything? I genuinely don't know, and the product never tells me.
- **What would make me lose trust?** Finding out the hard way, by losing my portfolio and recommendation history on a new device with no warning.
- **What would make me come back tomorrow?** N/A for this screen — but the *lack* of an answer here is itself a small trust tax I'm carrying into tomorrow's session.

## Would I personally keep using this every morning before the market opens?

**Cautiously, yes — for about a week, to see if the Morning Brief and the recommendation reasoning hold up.** The parts of the product that are real (the reasoning depth on Recommendations, the honesty in empty states and Settings, the "advisory only, simulated money" framing) are genuinely more trustworthy than most retail investing apps I've tried, which tend to oversell confidence. The two things that would make me quietly stop opening it: an empty Recommendations screen for several mornings in a row with no visible sign of *when* something will show up, and never learning whether my data survives a device switch. Neither is a dealbreaker on day one, but both are the kind of quiet friction that erodes a morning habit before I'd ever complain about it out loud.
