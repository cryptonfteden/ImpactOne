# ImpactOne Signature Moments & Rules — Phase DIFFERENTIATION-001

## Part 1 — The three unforgettable moments of a first session

These are not features to build; they are experiences that must be *guaranteed to occur* for any real first-time user, because ImpactOne's real architecture already makes each one possible — the discipline is ensuring the first session actually surfaces them rather than burying them.

### Moment 1 — "I watched it change its mind"

At some point in the first session, a user should see a real belief the platform holds — a Claim — visibly strengthen, weaken, or become contested, not as a silently-updated number but as a legible, attributed event: *this specific new evidence is why this changed.* No competitor reviewed offers this moment at all, because none of them treat their own output as a living, revisable belief. This is the single moment most likely to convert a skeptical first-time user into a trusting one, because it demonstrates honesty about uncertainty rather than asserting false stability.

**Why it matters:** it proves, experientially rather than through a claim in copy, that the platform is reasoning continuously rather than issuing a one-time verdict and moving on.

### Moment 2 — "It never ran out of real answers"

At some point in the first session, a user should ask "why" (directly, or by drilling into a claim) and be able to keep asking — through evidence, through counter-evidence, through original source — without ever hitting a dead end that reads as marketing copy or a generic disclaimer. The moment lands specifically when the user realizes the reasoning chain is *real all the way down*, not just real at the surface.

**Why it matters:** this is the single hardest thing for a competitor to fake, because faking it requires either genuine evidence infrastructure or being caught immediately by a curious user.

### Moment 3 — "It told me it didn't know"

At some point in the first session — ideally early, before the user has any reason to expect it — the platform should honestly say it doesn't have enough evidence to have an opinion about something, rather than manufacturing a confident-sounding answer. This should feel *surprising* the first time it happens, precisely because every other financial product a user has encountered defaults to sounding certain.

**Why it matters:** this is the single moment most likely to be remembered and retold — "the AI actually told me it didn't know" is a genuinely novel sentence in this category, and it is the emotional inverse of every competitor's design incentive.

## Part 2 — Ten permanent signature rules

Each rule below is written as a permanent constraint, not a current feature — several are already real, verified, structural facts in ImpactOne's architecture (confirmed in this engagement's own prior technical audits), which is precisely what makes this list a genuine, defensible moat rather than an aspirational style guide.

1. **No single number may ever represent both confidence and likelihood.** They are always computed independently and always shown as two distinguishable signals, never merged into one score.
2. **Every belief the platform holds is revisable, and every revision is visible.** Nothing is ever silently updated — a change in what the platform believes is itself a piece of information the user is shown.
3. **Every predictive output must expose what is known fact, what is inference, what is prediction, and what is uncertain** — every time, with no exceptions for a "simple" case.
4. **The platform is structurally forbidden from stating a trade action, a buy/sell/hold, or a position size**, enforced at the level of what data is even allowed to exist, not merely as a copywriting convention.
5. **Confidence degrades honestly when evidence is missing.** A component that can't be computed is excluded from the result, never defaulted to a plausible-looking mid-range guess.
6. **Every claim must be traceable to real, cited evidence, down to its original source** — an assertion with no traceable origin is not permitted to exist as a "finding."
7. **Nothing is shown with more numerical precision than the underlying data actually supports** — no fabricated decimal-point certainty dressing up a rough estimate.
8. **A single new piece of evidence can never swing a belief from one extreme to the other in one step.** Every update to confidence or probability is bounded, regardless of how dramatic the new evidence seems.
9. **Every ranking of "what matters" on any screen comes from one shared, canonical prioritization — never a screen-local reinvention of relevance**, so two different parts of the product can never disagree about what deserves attention.
10. **An empty or uncertain state always explains why and what would resolve it.** "No data" alone is never an acceptable terminal message anywhere in the product.

**Why competitors would struggle to replicate this list:** every rule above is a constraint on the *underlying reasoning system*, not on the interface painted over it. A competitor can copy a layout in days. Copying rules 1, 3, 4, 6, and 8 requires rebuilding how their AI output is generated and governed from the ground up — which is precisely why this list, not a visual style guide, is ImpactOne's real defensible differentiation.
