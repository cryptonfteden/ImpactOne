# Market Microstructure Guide — Concepts, Vocabulary, and Epistemic Boundaries

**Phase:** ALGORITHMIC-ACTIVITY-RESEARCH-001. Pure research/reference document — no production code was written. This is the conceptual foundation underlying `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_RESEARCH.md` (signal-by-signal research and vendor evaluation) and `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_SCORING.md` (the scoring model) — written so that anyone implementing, reviewing, or explaining this future agent to a user has one place to check a term's precise meaning and its real evidentiary limits, mirroring the role `TRUTH.md`/`EVIDENCE_QUALITY_MODEL.md` already play for this platform's epistemic vocabulary elsewhere.

## 1. The order book, quotes, and trades

- **Order book:** the live, standing collection of unexecuted limit orders resting at various prices, on both the bid (buy) and ask/offer (sell) side, for a given security on a given venue.
- **Quote:** the current best bid and best ask price (and, at L1, their sizes) — quotes update far more frequently than actual trades occur, since market participants continuously adjust resting orders in response to new information, other participants' activity, or their own inventory/risk management, most of which never results in an execution.
- **Trade / print:** an actual, completed execution — a match between a buy order and a sell order at a specific price and size, timestamped, and (where a vendor exposes it) tagged with the venue/exchange where it occurred and various condition codes (e.g., regular trade, odd lot, extended hours, subject to a later correction).
- **NBBO (National Best Bid and Offer):** the single best (highest) bid and best (lowest) ask across *all* U.S. exchanges at a given moment, consolidated by the SIP (Securities Information Processor) — the standard reference price for whether a trade executed "at, through, or away from" the prevailing market.
- **Fragmentation of U.S. equity markets:** unlike a single centralized exchange, U.S.-listed securities can trade simultaneously across 16+ registered exchanges plus off-exchange venues — this fragmentation is itself the structural reason execution algorithms exist at all (working a large order across many venues and over time, rather than dumping it on one exchange at once, meaningfully reduces market impact).

## 2. Venues: lit, dark, and reported

- **Lit exchanges:** venues (NYSE, NASDAQ, CBOE's family of exchanges, IEX, and others) where the order book (or at least the top of it) is publicly displayed in real time.
- **Dark pools / ATSs (Alternative Trading Systems):** venues where orders are *not* publicly displayed before execution — a trade only becomes visible after it has already occurred, reported to the consolidated tape after the fact, typically via a Trade Reporting Facility (TRF) tagged with a distinct venue code (commonly associated with FINRA's ADF — Alternative Display Facility). This is a completely legal, heavily regulated, and routine part of U.S. market structure (dark/off-exchange volume typically makes up a large minority — often 30-45% — of total consolidated volume for liquid names as a matter of ordinary market structure, not evidence of anything unusual on its own).
- **Reserve / hidden order types on *lit* exchanges:** distinct from a true dark-pool trade — a lit exchange can still allow an order to display only part of its size (an "iceberg"/"reserve" order), with the remainder hidden but still resting on that same lit exchange's own book. This is a different mechanism from an ATS trade, though both fall under the general umbrella of "hidden liquidity" in casual usage — this guide and `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_RESEARCH.md` treat them as two distinct signals (§2.3 iceberg vs. §2.4 dark-pool participation) precisely because their detection methods and required data tiers differ.

## 3. Order types relevant to algorithmic-activity detection

- **Intermarket Sweep Order (ISO):** a formally-defined Reg NMS order type that allows a participant to execute against multiple price levels/venues simultaneously without waiting for each venue's own order-protection rule to route the order elsewhere first — the official mechanism behind what this research calls a "liquidity sweep." Because ISOs are an explicit, defined order type (not just an inferred pattern), a vendor that exposes the ISO flag directly removes essentially all ambiguity from sweep detection — the single most precisely-defined concept in this whole research area.
- **Iceberg / reserve order:** described above (§2) — displays only a small visible "tip" of a much larger total order size, automatically refreshed as the tip is filled.
- **Odd lot:** a trade of fewer than 100 shares (the standard U.S. equity "round lot" size) — historically excluded or under-weighted in some consolidated-tape statistics, a real, well-documented data-quality nuance the SEC's own market-structure research explicitly studies (odd-lot activity has grown substantially with the rise of high-priced/mega-cap stocks and algorithmic slicing, making its historical under-representation in some tape statistics an increasingly material gap to be aware of).

## 4. Execution algorithm styles (context, not detection targets — per this mission's explicit framing)

- **VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) algorithm:** slices a parent order to track the security's own historical/predicted intraday volume curve (typically higher near the open and close, lower midday — a well-documented, U-shaped pattern), aiming for an average fill price near the day's true VWAP.
- **TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) algorithm:** slices a parent order into roughly equal pieces at regular time intervals, indifferent to volume — simpler than VWAP, and produces a more statistically regular (periodic) timing footprint as a result.
- **Participation-rate (POV) algorithm:** targets a fixed percentage of ongoing market volume in real time, rather than a pre-set historical curve — adaptively speeds up or slows down based on live volume, producing a footprint that sits conceptually between VWAP (curve-following) and a purely opportunistic/passive strategy.
- **Implementation-shortfall / arrival-price algorithm:** aims to minimize the difference between the price at order arrival and the actual average execution price, typically trading more aggressively early to reduce the risk of adverse price drift — a materially different, more urgency-weighted footprint than VWAP/TWAP.

**Important, mission-consistent framing:** this platform's own future Algorithmic Activity Agent should never claim to identify *which* of these styles (or which vendor/algo product) is in use — only that the *statistical footprint* of the tape is more consistent with one style's known signature (e.g., time-regular vs. volume-curve-following) than another. This is a description of observed statistical behavior, not an identification of a specific tool or actor.

## 5. Key statistical concepts used across the signal set

- **Autocorrelation / periodicity testing:** used to detect TWAP-style regular timing (§2.2 of the research doc) — a statistically well-established time-series technique, not a novel invention for this domain.
- **Herfindahl-style concentration index:** used to quantify trade/venue fragmentation (§2.10) — a standard concentration metric borrowed from industrial-organization economics, applied here to venue/trade-size distribution instead of market share.
- **Tick rule / quote rule (Lee-Ready methodology):** the standard, decades-old academic method for classifying a trade as buyer- or seller-initiated based on its execution price relative to the prevailing quote at the time — used in this research's `ExecutionPressure` gauge (`ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_SCORING.md` §1.4), not a novel classification scheme.
- **Order Book Imbalance (OBI):** `(bidSize − askSize) / (bidSize + askSize)` — one of the most heavily studied, empirically validated short-term microstructure metrics in the academic literature (the Cont/Kukanov/Stoikov line of research on order-book-event price impact is a well-known example), directly relevant to §2.8 of the research doc.
- **Message-to-trade / quote-to-trade / order-to-trade ratio:** a real, regulator-recognized market-quality metric — some European trading venues under MiFID II impose actual regulatory limits on this ratio, a strong external confirmation that it is treated as a legitimate, quantifiable concept by regulators, not merely an informal industry heuristic. The SEC's own MIDAS-derived public data (confirmed live, `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_RESEARCH.md` §3) explicitly computes and studies statistics in this same family.
- **Quote lifetime / hazard-and-survivor analysis:** the statistical study of how long a quote persists before being updated or cancelled — directly, confirmed-live, published by the SEC as its own "Quote Life Data Series," giving this platform a rare, free, official, ground-truth baseline for this specific concept rather than needing to estimate market-wide norms independently.

## 6. Scientifically defensible vs. marketing speculation — the central epistemic distinction

This mission explicitly requires this distinction to be made clearly. The table below is the single most important governance artifact in this whole research phase — every future implementation decision, every user-facing label, and every confidence-score ceiling in `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_SCORING.md` should trace back to this classification.

| Category | What belongs here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientifically defensible — directly measured, precisely defined** | Order Book Imbalance (§2.8); sweep detection where an ISO flag is present (§2.5); the labeled dark/lit venue split (§2.4); trade fragmentation statistics (§2.10); the honestly-scoped inter-trade-time "execution speed" statistic (§2.11) | Each of these is either a direct measurement of a precisely, often *officially* (Reg NMS, FINRA, SEC), defined quantity, or a straightforward arithmetic computation over real data — no inference about intent or identity is required to compute or interpret them. |
| **Scientifically defensible as a statistical pattern, but Moderate as an attribution** | VWAP-tracking (§2.1); TWAP-tracking (§2.2); the Institutional Execution composite (§2.12); quote-to-trade ratio abnormality (§2.9); sweep detection *without* an explicit ISO flag, inferred purely from cross-venue timestamp clustering | The underlying statistical measurement itself is real and rigorous, but the tape alone cannot fully rule out alternative, non-algorithmic explanations for the same observed pattern (e.g., many unrelated participants coincidentally trading near VWAP; a scheduled buyback program producing TWAP-like regularity for reasons unrelated to any trading algorithm). |
| **Real, documented phenomena — but genuinely Speculative to detect reliably as a general-purpose live signal from typical market data** | Quote stuffing without true L3 order-message data (§2.6); momentum ignition, at *any* data tier (§2.7); iceberg confirmation from trade-tape-only proxies without L2/L3 (§2.3) | Quote stuffing and momentum ignition are both genuinely documented in academic literature and, in some specific cases, SEC enforcement actions — they are not fabricated concepts. But detecting them *live, in general, for any security, from typical vendor data* requires either data most vendors do not provide (true L3 order messages) or direct evidence of *intent*, which no public market-data feed can ever supply, regardless of data tier or cost. |
| **Would be marketing speculation if presented as fact** | Any claim that a specific price move was "caused by algorithm X"; any claim that a specific participant or firm is "detected"; any claim that a pattern "proves" manipulation, front-running, or any other specific illegal conduct; presenting the Speculative-tier signals above (quote stuffing without L3, momentum ignition) at the same confidence level as the Scientifically-Defensible-tier signals | This is precisely the line this mission's own framing ("the goal is NOT to identify specific trading algorithms") draws, and this platform's own established governance (`canonicalVerdict.js`'s denylist, the Options/Valuation Agents' identical discipline) exists specifically to prevent a subsystem from overstating what it actually knows. Crossing this line would also carry real legal/reputational risk distinct from this platform's usual "don't fabricate a number" concern — accusing, even implicitly, a specific action of manipulation without genuine, legally-sufficient evidence is a materially more serious category of overreach than an ordinary confidence-inflation bug. |

### 6.1 The practical implication for every future user-facing surface

Any screen, Claim, or evidence record this future agent produces must:

1. Never name a specific algorithm, product, firm, or trader.
2. Never use causal language ("this caused," "this proves") for any Speculative-tier signal — only "consistent with."
3. Always show the underlying `scientificDefensibilityCeiling` (per `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_SCORING.md` §2.1) alongside any signal, not just a single number that could imply uniform rigor across a genuinely non-uniform evidence base.
4. Treat this whole domain as **evidence contributing to Claims/DecisionTrace**, exactly like the Options and Valuation Agents' own outputs — never a standalone verdict, and never itself setting or overriding `Recommendation.action`.

No code was written to implement any of the above — this document, together with `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_RESEARCH.md` and `ALGORITHMIC_ACTIVITY_SCORING.md`, is the design/decision record for whenever a real implementation phase begins, following the same "architecture → research → build" sequencing already proven for this platform's other new agent categories.
