# DUE_DILIGENCE_CHECKLIST.md

**Phase G2 — Investor Readiness Review**
**Purpose:** the exact, specific, independently-checkable evidence a seed investor would require before writing a check — after the 5-user beta actually runs. Every item is phrased so it can be answered yes/no/not-yet by pointing at a specific fact, never by a general impression. None of this is engineering — every item concerns a real person's real words or real behavior.

---

## Section A — Demand Is Real, Not Assumed

- [ ] At least 3 of the 5 beta users can each name a specific, real situation in their own life when they wanted this and didn't have it — in their own words, not a paraphrase.
- [ ] At least 1 of the 5 users opened the app on a day the founder did not prompt them to, and can describe why, unprompted.
- [ ] The founder can state, from memory, what each of the five people was actually trying to do the last time they used it — not a guess, an answer each person actually gave.

## Section B — Willingness to Pay Is Real, Not Inferred From Enthusiasm

- [ ] At least 1 of the 5 users, when asked directly, named an actual number they'd consider paying.
- [ ] At least 1 of the 5 users' stated enthusiasm and stated willingness to pay are checked against each other — and don't just assume they match.
- [ ] The founder can describe, specifically, what would have to change for a user who currently wouldn't pay to become one who would.

## Section C — Retention Is Observed, Not Self-Reported Only

- [ ] Actual daily-open counts exist for all 5 users across a full two-week window, attributable to a specific named individual (not a shared, unscoped account).
- [ ] At least one instance exists of a "say vs. do" mismatch being caught and followed up on personally — a user's stated trust and their actual behavior diverging, and the founder noticing and asking why.
- [ ] Zero users disengaged silently without the founder finding out why within the two-week window.

## Section D — Trust Has Actually Been Tested, Not Just Claimed

- [ ] At least one real instance occurred of the product being wrong about something, disclosed honestly, and the affected user's reaction is documented in their own words.
- [ ] Zero confirmed instances occurred of the product stating something false about a specific user's own data, portfolio, or account — checked directly against that user's real state, not assumed.
- [ ] At least one user can describe, correctly, in their own words, what the product does and does not do on their behalf (the "advisory only" boundary) — checked, not assumed understood.

## Section E — Word of Mouth Is Observed, Not Just Asked About

- [ ] At least one of the five users actually told a specific other real person about the product during the beta, independent of being asked "would you recommend this."
- [ ] At least one of the five users can name a specific person they would actively warn away from the product right now, and why.

## Section F — The Founder Personally Did the Work

- [ ] The founder personally conducted a live conversation (not a written form) with every one of the five users at each of the three planned checkpoints.
- [ ] The founder can recall, without notes, one specific, memorable detail about each of the five people's experience.
- [ ] A record exists (even informal) of what changed, specifically, as a direct result of something a beta user said — not a general "we made improvements" claim.

## Section G — The Product Can Actually Support Five Distinct People

- [ ] Confirmed (independently, not taken on the founder's word) that each of the five users' portfolio, profile, and feedback data stayed separate from the other four's throughout the beta — no shared-account contamination occurred.
- [ ] If any cross-contamination did occur, it is disclosed plainly, along with what it revealed about the risk of running a beta at all before this was fixed.

---

## How This Checklist Is Scored

Every item above should be marked with a specific fact, quote, number, or "not yet available" — never a checkmark based on general confidence. A due-diligence checklist half-answered with impressions instead of facts is worse than an honestly incomplete one, because it creates false confidence exactly where a real check should exist.
