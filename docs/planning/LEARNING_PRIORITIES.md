# LEARNING_PRIORITIES.md

**Phase G1 — Beta Success Review**
**Purpose:** the ten most valuable insights the founder should walk away with after two weeks with five beta users, ranked by how much they should shape what happens next. If time runs short during any interview, protect the conversations that surface the top-ranked insights first.

---

### 1. Would any of them actually pay, and what would feel fair?
Every other insight is secondary to this one. A product that five people enjoy but wouldn't pay for is a hobby, not a business. This must be asked directly (Day 14, question 2), not inferred from enthusiasm — enthusiasm and willingness to pay diverge constantly, and the gap between them is exactly what a beta this small exists to catch before it costs real money to discover later.

### 2. What specific real-life moment made each person actually open the app, unprompted?
This is the closest a founder can get to observing genuine need rather than polite interest. Five real, specific triggers ("I opened it because my sister texted me about a stock" / "I opened it Sunday night before the market opened because I was anxious") tell the founder more about who to reach next and how to describe the product than any amount of feature feedback.

### 3. Did stated trust match actual behavior, for each person, individually?
The single highest-leverage diagnostic in the whole beta. A person who says "I trust it" but stopped opening it, or who says "it's confusing" but opened it every single day anyway, is telling the founder something their words alone would hide. Track this divergence by name, not as an average.

### 4. Who would each person actively warn a specific friend away from this, and why?
More diagnostic than "would you recommend," because it forces specificity and honesty in a way the positive version doesn't. The reasons people give here are usually the sharpest, most useful piece of product feedback the entire beta produces.

### 5. Did anyone quietly disengage without ever saying anything was wrong?
The most dangerous failure mode is silent, not vocal. A user who stops opening the app without complaining has usually already decided it isn't for them — catching this in real time (via the Day 7 conversation, and by simply noticing before Day 14) and asking why, personally and immediately, is worth more than reading every other signal correctly.

### 6. What did each person believe the product would never do — and was that belief right or wrong?
This tells the founder whether the product's actual boundaries (what it does and doesn't do, especially "advisory only, never trades for you") were communicated clearly, or whether people are carrying incorrect assumptions that could become a serious trust problem the moment they're tested.

### 7. What's the one thing each person would change if they could change only one?
Forcing a single answer (rather than an open-ended wishlist) reveals what actually mattered enough to remember after two weeks of real use, filtering out the noise of minor annoyances that don't actually threaten anything.

### 8. Did using it change how anxious or confident each person feels about their own money?
For a company whose stated identity is building financial confidence, not just providing information, this may be the most mission-critical question in the whole beta — and it's the one question a features-and-bugs mindset is most likely to forget to ask.

### 9. What did each person compare this to in their own head — a competitor, a friend, the news, or nothing at all?
The real competitive set is whatever a user was actually doing before, not whatever the founder assumes it is. This shapes how the product should be positioned and described far more reliably than guessing.

### 10. What did each person hold back from saying until directly asked at the very end?
The Day 14 guide's final question ("was there anything you were afraid to tell me because it felt too minor or too harsh") exists specifically to surface this. Whatever comes out here is very often the single most useful piece of feedback in the entire two weeks, precisely because it's the thing a polite person almost didn't say.

---

## How to Use This Ranking

If the beta produces only five clean answers to insight #1 and nothing else, it was still worth running. If it produces detailed dashboards on every metric in `BETA_SUCCESS_METRICS.md` but the founder can't answer insight #1 with confidence for even one of the five people, the beta was run in a way that measured everything except the thing that actually matters.
