## 1. 13F filings — the regulatory foundation for this entire research area

- **Required data/mechanism:** SEC Form 13F is required of institutional investment managers with at least $100 million in qualifying assets under management, filed **quarterly**, due **45 calendar days** after each calendar quarter's end. It discloses the manager's **long** equity positions in a specific SEC-designated list of "13(f) securities" (predominantly U.S.-listed common stock) as of the **last day of the quarter** — since 2013, submitted in a real, structured, machine-readable XML "information table" format.
- **This is, by a meaningful margin, the single most severe freshness/reporting-delay limitation encountered anywhere in this entire research series so far — the headline finding of this whole phase.** A position reported in a given 13F reflects reality as of a date that is **already up to 90 days old at the moment of filing**, and the filing itself is not made public until up to **45 additional days** after that — meaning the *most current* 13F-derived data point available at any moment can be **up to ~135 days stale**, and a position a fund fully exited the day after the snapshot date would not be reflected as closed until the *next* quarter's filing (up to another ~135 days later). This is materially worse than the ETF Flow research's already-significant SEC Form N-PORT ~60-day delay (`ETF_FLOW_RESEARCH.md` §11) and must be disclosed with equal or greater prominence.
- **A second, real, equally important coverage limitation, distinct from the freshness issue:** 13F covers **only long equity positions** in the designated security list — it does **not** disclose short positions, most derivatives/options exposure, non-U.S. securities, bonds, or cash. A fund's true net view on a stock (e.g., a large reported long position that is substantially hedged via undisclosed short derivatives) can be **materially different** from what 13F data alone shows — this must be disclosed as a real, structural limitation of the data itself, not something better data processing could ever fix.
- **Scientifically defensible?** **Yes, as a real, mandated regulatory disclosure with real legal weight** — but its practical usefulness as a *timely* signal is intrinsically bounded by the two limitations above, a real, disclosed constraint on the entire research area, not a data-quality problem specific to any one vendor.

## 2. Institutional ownership

- **Required data:** the aggregate share count/dollar value held across **every** 13F-filing institution holding a given stock, divided by the stock's total shares outstanding.
- **Reliability:** high, as a lagging, quarter-end-snapshot metric — genuinely useful for understanding a stock's *typical* institutional profile, not for detecting anything that happened recently.
- **False-positive risks:** conflating "high institutional ownership" with "institutionally endorsed" — a high percentage can simply reflect broad passive/index-fund ownership (directly connecting to `ETF_FLOW_RESEARCH.md` §5-6's own passive-investing/index-inclusion findings) rather than active, conviction-driven institutional interest; this platform's own Exposure Score design for ETF ownership (`ETF_FLOW_SCORING_MODEL.md` §5) is a directly relevant, reusable precedent for treating raw ownership percentage as *contextual*, not directional.
- **Scientifically defensible?** **Yes**, as a descriptive statistic; the *interpretation* (passive vs. active ownership) requires real, disclosed care.

## 3. Ownership concentration

- **Required data:** the same aggregate holdings data as §2, but examined for **distribution** across the holding institutions (a few very large holders vs. many small ones), rather than just the aggregate total.
- **Reliability:** directly reuses the Herfindahl-style concentration-index approach already established and reused repeatedly across this research series (`TECHNICAL_SIGNAL_PRIORITY.md`'s venue concentration, `SENTIMENT_SCORING_MODEL.md`'s source diversity) — the same well-established statistical tool, applied here to institutional holders instead.
- **False-positive risks:** a highly concentrated ownership base (a few large holders) is neither inherently bullish nor bearish on its own — it is a real, useful *contextual* fact (e.g., a stock whose float is dominated by a handful of large holders may show more exaggerated price moves if any one of them meaningfully changes its position) that should inform confidence/risk framing, not be presented as a standalone directional signal.
- **Scientifically defensible?** **Yes**, as a descriptive concentration statistic — the same well-established technique used elsewhere in this platform, not a novel invention for this domain.

## 4. Fund accumulation / 5. Fund distribution

- **Required data:** a **direct comparison of the same fund's position in the same stock across two consecutive quarterly 13F filings** — a real, directly computable delta (accumulation = an increasing position quarter-over-quarter; distribution = a decreasing one).
- **Reliability:** high in terms of raw computation, but inherits §1's full freshness limitation — an "accumulation" signal detected today describes a decision the fund actually made up to ~135 days ago, not a current one.
- **False-positive risks:** a single quarter's change can reflect routine portfolio rebalancing, client redemption/inflow-driven scaling (a fund growing or shrinking its AUM overall, proportionally scaling every position without any change in per-stock conviction), or a mechanical index-tracking adjustment — none of which reflect a genuine, stock-specific view change. This directly motivates §7's Portfolio Turnover concept as a necessary moderating/contextual check, not a standalone confirmation.
- **Scientifically defensible?** **Yes**, as a directly computable delta between two real, disclosed data points — the interpretation risk lies entirely in distinguishing genuine conviction-driven change from routine portfolio mechanics (§7).

## 6. New positions / Closed positions

- **Required data:** the presence of a stock in the current quarter's 13F for a given fund, absent from the immediately prior quarter's (a "new position"), or the reverse (a "closed position").
- **Reliability:** directly computable, but with a real, disclosed ambiguity for "closed" positions specifically: a position's disappearance from a 13F can mean a genuine, complete sale — **or** it can mean the position fell below a reporting *de minimis* threshold (certain small positions may not require full itemization in some 13F contexts), **or**, more rarely, that the fund itself stopped filing (e.g., dropped below the $100M AUM threshold, wound down, or was acquired). This ambiguity should be disclosed explicitly, not silently assumed to always mean "fully sold."
- **False-positive risks:** treating every "closed position" as an unambiguous, high-conviction bearish signal without acknowledging the real alternative explanations above.
- **Scientifically defensible?** **Yes** for the "presence/absence" computation itself; **the interpretation of *why* a position closed carries real, disclosed uncertainty** that should be reflected in a lower confidence weighting for closed-position signals relative to accumulation/distribution signals with clearer, unambiguous continuing positions.

## 7. Portfolio turnover

- **Required data:** the fund's own overall portfolio composition change rate, computed across **all** of that fund's reported positions between two consecutive quarters — a fund-level statistic, not a stock-specific one.
- **Reliability:** directly, and importantly, **moderates** how much weight a specific stock-level accumulation/distribution signal from that fund deserves — a rare, deliberate position change from a historically low-turnover, buy-and-hold-style fund is meaningfully more informative evidence than the same magnitude of change from a fund that reshuffles its entire portfolio every quarter as a matter of routine style. This directly reuses this whole research series' established "correlation/context discounting" principle (e.g., `UNIFIED_SCORING_MODEL.md`'s `independenceFactor`, `ETF_FLOW_SCORING_MODEL.md`'s Exposure Score), applied here as a **fund-level conviction-context discount**.
- **Scientifically defensible?** **Yes** — this is standard, transparent arithmetic (a real turnover-rate calculation used broadly in fund-analysis practice), not a novel technique.

## 8. Smart money tracking — the single most epistemically delicate topic in this entire research area

- **The real, honest methodological problem, stated plainly:** 13F data discloses **positions only** — it contains **no performance/return data whatsoever**. Establishing which funds are genuinely "smart" (i.e., have a real, statistically demonstrable track record of skillful stock-picking) requires independently sourced fund **performance** data compared against a real benchmark over a real, sufficient sample of history — data this platform does not have and 13F itself cannot provide.
- **The real, practical consequence:** most retail-facing "smart money"/"whale tracking" products (including the real vendors evaluated in §9 below) substantially rely on **name recognition and reputation** (well-known billionaire investors, famous activist funds) rather than a rigorously, independently verified track record specific to the platform doing the labeling — a real, important risk of exactly the "scientifically defensible vs. marketing speculation" boundary this whole research series has been built around (`MARKET_MICROSTRUCTURE_GUIDE.md` §6's identical framework, directly applicable here).
- **A real, honest, defensible path forward, consistent with this platform's own established discipline:** any "smart money" designation this platform ever applies to a specific fund should be **explicitly earned from this platform's own accumulated, graded outcome history** (reusing, not duplicating, the already-real `Outcome`/`calibrationReportService.js` infrastructure this whole research series has repeatedly recommended reusing) — i.e., only label a fund "historically accurate" once this platform has itself tracked that fund's disclosed positions against real subsequent stock performance over a genuine, sample-size-gated period, never inherited from external reputation alone.
- **Scientifically defensible?** **The underlying position-tracking data is real and defensible; the "smart money" labeling concept itself is currently more reputation-based marketing framing than a rigorously earned, platform-verified claim, unless and until this platform builds its own outcome-tracking history for it** — this is the single most important epistemic caution in this whole research phase, and should be disclosed with real prominence in `INSTITUTIONAL_SCORING_MODEL.md`'s Smart Money Score design.

## 9. Reporting delays (restated, consolidated)

Directly consolidating §1's finding: the ~45-day post-quarter filing deadline, combined with the quarter-end-snapshot nature of the underlying data itself, produces a real, structural, unavoidable staleness of up to ~135 days for the most current available 13F data point at any given moment — a permanent characteristic of this entire data domain, not a fixable latency problem, directly analogous in *kind* (though more severe in *degree*) to the ETF Flow research's SEC Form N-PORT ~60-day delay finding.
