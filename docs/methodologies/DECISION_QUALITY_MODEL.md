# Decision Quality Model
## Office of the Chief Product Strategist — ImpactOne

**Mandate:** Define how ImpactOne objectively measures whether it is helping users make *better long-term decisions* — a fundamentally different, and harder, question than whether the platform's own predictions were correct. A user can make an excellent decision and still experience a bad outcome, because markets are genuinely uncertain. A user can make a poor decision and still get lucky. This model exists to measure the decision, not just the result — the same distinction `INVESTMENT_INTELLIGENCE_MODEL.md` draws between a mechanism being right and a call being right.

---

## Why Outcome Alone Is the Wrong Measure

If this platform only ever measured whether a user's portfolio went up, it would be rewarding luck as often as judgment, and would have no way to tell the difference. A user who held through volatility exactly as their own stated risk tolerance intended, and lost money anyway because the world was genuinely uncertain, made a *good* decision. A user who abandoned a sound plan in a moment of panic and got lucky made a *bad* decision that happened to work out. Measuring only the scoreboard teaches this platform, and its users, the wrong lesson — exactly the same trap `TRUTH.md` warns against when it comes to grading the platform's own calls, applied here to a user's calls about their own money.

---

## The Five Components of Decision Quality

### 1. Process Adherence
**What it measures:** Did the user's actual action match their own previously stated risk tolerance, horizon, and the platform's sizing guidance — regardless of what happened afterward?
**Why it matters:** A decision that was consistent with a thoughtfully stated plan is a good decision at the moment it was made, independent of the outcome. Consistent adherence, tracked over time, is the strongest available proxy for genuine decision quality.

### 2. Behavioral Discipline Under Stress
**What it measures:** During periods of real volatility or a market downturn, did the user hold to a position consistent with their plan, or deviate in a way that matches classic panic behavior (selling into a decline with no invalidation condition actually triggered, or chasing a rally with no new evidence)?
**Why it matters:** This is the single most common way good long-term decisions get destroyed in practice — not by bad initial judgment, but by abandoning sound judgment under emotional pressure. A platform that can help a user notice this pattern in their own behavior, honestly and without judgment, is doing exactly the job this entire company exists to do.

### 3. Comprehension Growth
**What it measures:** Is the user's understanding of core concepts (confidence, uncertainty, risk, exposure) demonstrably increasing over time — fewer repeated "explain this" taps, more independent engagement with evidence rather than relying solely on the verdict?
**Why it matters:** A user who understands more over time is positioned to make better decisions for the rest of their life, independent of any single call this platform ever makes — this is the most durable, most important form of value the product can create.

### 4. Calibration of the User's Own Judgment
**What it measures:** Where a user is willing to state their own expectation before an outcome is known (an optional, lightweight prompt — "how do you think this will turn out?"), how well does their own stated confidence match what actually happens over time?
**Why it matters:** This is the clearest, most direct signal that the platform is teaching genuine calibrated thinking, not just delivering verdicts to be passively accepted — a user whose own predictions become better calibrated over a year of use is the single strongest evidence this platform is making people better decision-makers, not just better-informed ones.

### 5. Long-Run, Risk-Adjusted Portfolio Outcome
**What it measures:** Real portfolio performance against a relevant benchmark, over a long enough horizon that genuine skill or discipline has a chance to separate from short-term noise — deliberately weighted lowest of the five components, and never presented without the other four alongside it.
**Why it matters:** Outcome still matters — it is not ignored — but it is one input among five, explicitly prevented from being the only one, for the same reason `INVESTMENT_INTELLIGENCE_MODEL.md` refuses to grade a thesis by its price outcome alone.

---

## How the Five Components Combine

They are never collapsed into a single "Decision Quality Score." Per the same discipline `TRUST_SCORE_MODEL.md` applies to trust, each component is tracked and shown separately, because a user who is high on Process Adherence and Behavioral Discipline but currently experiencing a poor Long-Run Outcome is a fundamentally different, and considerably less concerning, case than a user who is low on all five. Collapsing them into one number would erase exactly the distinction this entire model exists to preserve.

---

## What This Model Is Never Used For

- It is never shown to a user as a judgmental "grade" on their own decisions — it exists to help the platform understand whether it is genuinely helping, not to score or shame individual users.
- It never substitutes for the platform's own calibration measurement (`OUTCOME_INTELLIGENCE_ENGINE.md`) — that measures whether the platform's calls were right; this measures whether the user's decisions, informed by those calls, were sound.
- It is never used to justify more aggressive engagement tactics under the banner of "helping users decide better" — a mechanic that improves this model's numbers by increasing anxiety-driven checking has failed the model's actual purpose, regardless of what the numbers show.

---

## The Ultimate Test of Whether This Model Is Working

If, over a period of years, users of this platform show rising Process Adherence, rising Behavioral Discipline under stress, rising Comprehension, and increasingly well-calibrated personal judgment — with Long-Run Outcome simply following as a natural consequence rather than being chased directly — this platform has succeeded at the actual mission stated in `VISION.md`: not telling people what to do with their money, but helping them decide for themselves, with real evidence, better than they could have on their own.
