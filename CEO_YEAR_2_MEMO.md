# CEO — Year 2 Memo
## To the Company, at the Start of Year 2

Team,

A year ago, we didn't have a single real user. We had a product that looked good in a demo and a set of principles we hadn't yet been tested against. This memo is not a victory lap — it's a clear-eyed look at what Year 1 actually proved, and what Year 2 has to do differently.

---

## What Year 1 Actually Proved

We proved that our own review process works — we found our worst problems ourselves, before a real user ever had to. We proved that when we build carefully, the result is genuinely excellent: the Recommendations experience we shipped is something I would put in front of anyone, professional or beginner, without flinching. And we proved, the hard way, that fixing the right thing in the wrong place doesn't count — a beautifully rebuilt screen sitting next to an unfixed, less-visible one is still an unfixed product, and we spent real time this year learning that lesson at our own expense before any user had to learn it for us.

We did not yet prove that we can sustain a real, multi-year honest track record — because we haven't had a year of real users to build one from yet. That is the actual work of Year 2, not a continuation of Year 1's work. It is the beginning of the only asset this company actually has a long-term moat in.

---

## Execution

Year 2 has one execution standard, unchanged from every document we've written about it: **a Critical finding blocks everything until it's closed, on every screen, not just the ones we happen to be looking at that week.** We got this half right in Year 1 — we found problems fast and fixed them fast, but we fixed them in the place we happened to be working, not everywhere the same problem existed. This year, when we find something wrong, the first question is always "where else does this same problem exist," not just "is this specific instance fixed."

## Trust

Our trust score, our calibration error, and our resilience after a disclosed mistake are the three numbers I will ask about every single week this year — not signups, not session count. If those three numbers are healthy and our growth numbers are behind schedule, that is a good week. If our growth numbers are ahead of schedule and those three are not healthy, that is a bad week, regardless of what the rest of the dashboard says. This isn't a slogan — it's how I am actually going to run these reviews, starting with the first one.

## Learning

We now have real infrastructure for this — evidence quality, outcome grading, World Memory, a learning system that keeps user feedback and market evidence from contaminating each other. Year 2's job is to actually feed it real data and read what comes back honestly, including when what comes back says we were wrong about something we believed. The whole point of building this infrastructure in Year 1 was to be able to say, honestly, in Year 2 and every year after, exactly how well-calibrated we actually are — not to have built an impressive-sounding system we then quietly avoid checking against reality.

## Product

The product roadmap for Year 2 is not a wish list — it's the specific, ordered set of gaps between what we already proved we can build (the Recommendations experience) and what most of our users actually see first (Daily Feed, Home, onboarding). Nothing new gets built until that gap closes everywhere it currently exists. We know exactly what "good" looks like now, because we already built it once. Year 2's job is consistency, not invention.

---

## The One Thing I Want Everyone to Remember This Year

We are not trying to win by being the most exciting app in this category. Every competitor who could beat us on excitement, speed, or scale already exists, and we've been explicit with ourselves about exactly where we will never try to compete with them. We win, if we win at all, by being the company that was still honest about its own mistakes in year ten the same way it was in month one. Nothing we do this year is worth doing if it trades against that. Let's go build a real track record — our first one.

— CEO
