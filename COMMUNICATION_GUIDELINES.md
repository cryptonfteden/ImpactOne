# Communication Guidelines
## Office of the Chief Customer Officer — The Writing Style of ImpactOne

**Mandate:** Define how every sentence inside this product is written. Two rules override every other guideline below and are never negotiable: **never exaggerate, and never create false urgency.** A sentence that violates either rule does not ship, regardless of how it tests, how it reads, or how much more "engaging" it might be.

---

## The Standard Every Sentence Is Held To

Before any specific rule below, every sentence in this product must pass one test: **could a careful, skeptical reader accuse this sentence of overstating what's actually known?** If yes, it is rewritten until the answer is no. This is not a tone preference — it is the same epistemic standard `TRUTH.md` sets for the platform's reasoning, applied one layer up, to the words themselves.

---

## Confidence

**Rule:** State confidence as a specific number with a plain-language anchor, never as an adjective alone, and never rounded up to sound more decisive than the number itself supports.

- **Do:** "Confidence: 72/100 — the evidence for this view is fairly strong, though not certain."
- **Don't:** "We're confident about this." (No number. No honest ceiling on that confidence.)
- **Don't:** "This is very likely to happen." (Implies certainty a probabilistic score doesn't support, without stating the actual number.)

---

## Uncertainty

**Rule:** State uncertainty as plainly and with as much visual and verbal weight as confidence — never as a footnote, a disclaimer, or an afterthought.

- **Do:** "Uncertainty: 45/100 — there's real disagreement in the evidence for this view."
- **Don't:** "Please note results may vary." (Vague, legalistic, communicates nothing specific.)
- **Don't:** Omitting uncertainty entirely because the confidence number alone "sounds complete."

---

## Risk

**Rule:** State risk in the same size, position, and tone as any corresponding upside — never smaller, never later, never softened with hedging language upside doesn't receive.

- **Do:** "Estimated downside: -12% if this view is wrong, based on a stated stop level." (Same visual and structural weight as the stated upside.)
- **Don't:** "There is some risk involved, as with any investment." (Generic boilerplate that discloses nothing specific.)
- **Don't:** Burying the risk figure below the fold while the upside figure leads the screen.

---

## Education

**Rule:** Explain a concept once, plainly, in the fewest words that are actually true — never condescending, never padded with unnecessary caveats that make a simple idea sound complicated.

- **Do:** "Confidence measures how strong the evidence is. Uncertainty measures how much disagreement exists. A claim can be strong and still genuinely contested — that's why we show both."
- **Don't:** "As you may or may not be aware, in the complex world of modern finance, there are many factors that can influence outcomes..." (Padding that delays the actual explanation.)
- **Don't:** Explaining a concept identically every single time a user has already demonstrated they understand it — education should fade once earned, per `MOBILE_PRODUCT_MASTERPLAN.md` §9.

---

## Warnings

**Rule:** State exactly what could go wrong and what specifically to watch for — never a vague caution that protects the platform legally without informing the user practically.

- **Do:** "This call would be proven wrong if supporting data fails to confirm this move, or if sector leadership rotates away from these holdings."
- **Don't:** "Investing involves risk and past performance is not indicative of future results." (True, generic, and communicates nothing this specific user can act on.)

---

## Success

**Rule:** Acknowledge a correct call calmly and specifically — never with celebratory language disproportionate to a financial outcome, and never in a way that implies the platform wants to be thanked or credited more than it wants to be understood.

- **Do:** "This call was graded correct: the position moved as expected, roughly in line with the stated confidence."
- **Don't:** "🎉 Great call! You're on a roll!" (Gamifies a financial outcome and implies a pattern from a single data point.)

---

## Failure

**Rule:** Acknowledge an incorrect call with the same calm, specific tone as a success — never smaller, never later, never explained away with excuses that shift blame from the platform's own reasoning.

- **Do:** "This call was graded incorrect: the expected move did not materialize within the stated horizon. Here's what we think went wrong, specifically."
- **Don't:** "Unfortunately, market conditions didn't cooperate this time." (Vague, blame-shifting, tells the user nothing they can actually learn from.)

---

## Words and Phrases Never Used in This Product

- "Guaranteed," "sure thing," "can't miss," or any equivalent implying certainty a probabilistic system cannot honestly claim.
- "Don't miss out," "act now," "limited time," or any manufactured-urgency framing not tied to a genuine, stated, time-bound fact.
- "Everyone is buying," "the smart money is doing X," or any appeal to crowd behavior as if it were evidence on its own.
- Superlatives with no specific backing ("the best," "the smartest") — a claim like this is only usable when a specific, checkable number sits directly next to it.
- Exclamation points on any sentence describing money, risk, or a recommendation.

---

## The Test for Every New Sentence Before It Ships

1. Does it state a specific number wherever a number is the honest answer, rather than a vague adjective?
2. Does it give risk the same weight, size, and position as upside?
3. Does it avoid every word or phrase in the banned list above?
4. Would a careful, skeptical reader find nothing in it to accuse of exaggeration?
5. Would this same sentence still be published, unedited, if it described a mistake rather than a success?

A sentence that fails any of these five questions is rewritten before it ships — this is a hard gate, not a style suggestion, for the same reason `TRUTH.md` treats its own principles as non-negotiable: the words are the only place most users will ever actually meet the platform's honesty, and a beautifully-designed screen with one exaggerated sentence on it has already failed.
